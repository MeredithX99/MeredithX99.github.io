What mainly affects my happy mood?

Hypothesis: The more tasks I have done, the happier I am.
Furthermore, the more steps I have taken, the happier I am.
Additionally, playing at home has a negative impact on my happiness, while studying and going out both have positive impacts on my happiness.

Explain:

In this visualization, it can be observed that my data support my hypotheses.

Firstly, as the scatter gets further to the left side, the circle becomes larger & redder, which indicates that the lower the percentage of playing at home, the happier I am.
In addition, the closer the scatter is to the lower-left corner, more circles are larger and redder; the higher the scatter is, more circles are larger and redder. This shows that "study" and "go outside" both have positive effects on happy mood.

Secondly, the more sectors in the circle, the larger & redder the circle becomes, which indicates that the more tasks completed, the happier I am.

Thirdly, the circle where the dark purple plane is located tends to be small & the red is very light, which indicates that the more steps I have taken, the happier I am.

Conclusion:

Both the tasks done and the number of steps taken have a positive impact on my happiness, and the higher the percentage of the day devoted to studying and going outside, the happier I am. Playing at home has a negative effect on my happiness.

Changes:

About position of coordinates:

As opposed to "attempt1", I changed the way each activity is presented as a percentage of the day.
0%-100% ranges from the height of the triangle to the sides of the triangle. I think this is now easier for others to understand and visually position.
It also makes up for the previous inability to label the axes in the graph (in the triangles) (because the previous axes were in the middle and would overlap with the data points).

About why do not use "lineScale"

It is very complicated to control the position of rotation of Axisline. When used for the sides I needed to rotate it and it was also difficult to tune it to a very accurate position.

Challenge:

When I draw data points in equilateral triangles, I cannot use the height of the triangle as the xy axis to locate the data points. So I switched my thinking and used the vertex of the lower left corner as the origin to draw the data points. This required me to figure out the coordinate values of each data point relative to this origin, so I used mathematical knowledge to figure out the true x-values of the data points.
